My name is Morgan Voltz. Before I wrote my first real line of code, I spent some fifteen years in hands-on work, and that detour shapes the way I work today.

## A path that did not start with software

I studied electronics for five years: my first programs were C for microcontrollers, and my first logic gates date from then. I then spent about fifteen years in the field, mostly in German-speaking Switzerland, working in German every day, as an electrician on access control, security and fire detection systems, up to supervising construction sites. I later became an automation technician. I taught myself programming again, out of frustration at not finding tools that fitted what I needed. When the opportunity came, I chose to go back to university to become a full-stack developer: a personal choice, not one steered by school, and I present it as a strength rather than a detour to justify.

## What I like to build

Learning a new language or a new framework does not scare me: that is the whole point of being full-stack. Day to day I use C#, Rust, PHP, Python, JavaScript or TypeScript, with React, xUnit or Dioxus depending on the project. A bug or a problem I cannot solve yet is a challenge before it is an obstacle. Alongside the projects of my training I run Kovelt, my company, where I build Vefta, a productivity suite written in Rust; I also contribute to open source tools.

## How I work

I am used to Clean Architecture and hexagonal architecture, and I take a close interest in microservices. Before I start working with an AI agent (Claude Code first, though I can adapt to others), I write ADRs (architecture decision records) that settle the project's architecture, stack and security, so that the agent never drifts from the brief: a strict method that lets me move fast without losing quality. I also rely on these agents for security audits, for hunting down monoliths and, in lower-level languages, for catching memory corruption. I test systematically, the architecture itself included, so that an automated check enforces the rules rather than a mere honour system. I work in an agile way, in class as in personal projects, with Jira, GitLab, Slack and the other Atlassian tools; I host my own GitLab, Nextcloud and Penpot on a personal VPS. I also keep learning every week by following technology developments, with ongoing subscriptions to Udemy and OpenClassrooms.

## What I am looking for

I am preparing the Licence professionnelle Métiers de l'informatique (professional Bachelor's degree, EQF level 6) at UHA, on the UHA 4.0 programme, which comprises three DU (university diploma) stages, 4.0.1 to 4.0.3. I am currently in DU 4.0.2, and the Licence is awarded on completion of DU 4.0.3, in 2028. I then aim for the work-study Master's in Computer Science and Mobility: artificial intelligence and software architecture interest me equally. I am looking for a six-month internship from January 2027, then a work-study programme for the rest of the course, a rhythm that leaves time for classes and for my own projects.
